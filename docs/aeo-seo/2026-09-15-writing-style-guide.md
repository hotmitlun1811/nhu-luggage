# Stow Writing Style Guide — Natural, Native, Globally Clear English

**Status:** Active standard for all `/guides/*` and site copy, from 2026-09-15.
**Why it exists:** the owner flagged the first rich-guide drafts as over-written — too many em-dashes, long winding sentences, clever/literary phrasing that buries the point, indirect openings. It read effortful, almost translated. This guide fixes that.

**The through-line:** short sentences, active voice, common words, the point first, concrete facts, and restraint with dashes and asides. That combination is at the same time what *sounds native* AND what *reads easily for the non-native travelers* who make up much of Stow's audience — and it is what Google and AI answer engines extract. The three goals do not conflict.

Sourced from primary authorities: [plainlanguage.gov / Digital.gov](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles/short-simple), the [GOV.UK content style guide](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/), [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/), the [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/global-communications/writing-tips) and [Google](https://developers.google.com/style/translation) global-writing style guides, and Kohl's *Global English Style Guide*. Full research trail: `scratchpad/research/native-global-english-style.md` (session).

## Numeric targets (pin every draft to these)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Average sentence length | **15–20 words** |
| Max sentence length | **~25 words** — split anything longer |
| Joined clauses per sentence | **≤2** |
| Paragraph length | **≤3 sentences**, one idea each (5 absolute max) |
| Flesch Reading Ease | **60–70** (plain English) |
| Reading grade level | **Grade 6–8** |
| Em-dashes | **≤1 per paragraph** — prefer a period or comma |
| Answer paragraph under a heading | **40–60 words**, the answer in sentence one |
| Length vs. first draft | Cut **~50%** |

Free check: paste into **hemingwayapp.com** — aim for grade ≤8 and few or no red (very hard) sentences.

## The 12 rules

1. **Answer first.** Put the point in sentence one. No throat-clearing ("It is important to note that…", "When it comes to…").
2. **One idea per sentence.** Average 15–20 words, hard cap ~25.
3. **Active voice, named doer.** "You collect your bag," not "The bag can be collected."
4. **Talk to "you," in the present tense.** This is what makes it sound like a person, not a manual.
5. **Common word over fancy.** buy > purchase, use > utilize, about > approximately, start > commence, help > assist, enough > sufficient.
6. **Cut fillers and hedges.** Delete very, really, quite, actually, totally, somewhat, arguably, in order to.
7. **Verbs, not noun-blobs.** decide (not "make a decision"), store (not "provide storage for").
8. **≤1 em-dash per paragraph; no nested parentheticals.** If an aside matters, make it its own sentence.
9. **≤2 clauses per sentence.** Three "and/but/or" joins = split it.
10. **Concrete, not literary.** Give the number, the minute, the đồng — not "affordably priced" or "a stone's throw away."
11. **Keep modifiers next to what they modify; no long noun strings.** "secure luggage storage at the airport," not "airport secure long-term luggage storage facility."
12. **Cut to half, then chunk.** Short paragraphs, headings, bullets, bold the key fact.

## Global-audience rules (many readers are non-native)

- **No idioms or figurative phrases.** "a stone's throw" → "a 4-minute walk"; also kill no-brainer, off the beaten path, bang for your buck, hit the ground running.
- **Prefer single-word verbs to ambiguous phrasal verbs.** drop off → **leave**; pick up → **collect**; sort out → **arrange**; check out → **leave (a hotel)**. Keep the universal ones: set up, log in, check in.
- **No humor, sarcasm, or irony as load-bearing.** Warmth comes from being helpful and direct, not witty.
- **No culture-bound or seasonal references.** Da Nang is tropical — use months, not "spring/fall." No sports metaphors, no country-specific holidays.
- **Unambiguous numbers.** Dates: **15 September 2026** (never 09/15/26). Money: **150,000 VND (about US$6)** — always the currency code, never bare "$6" or "150k". Times: **7:00 AM–10:00 PM**.
- **One term per concept.** Always "luggage storage" — never rotate through bag drop / left-luggage / baggage hold. Helps non-natives *and* SEO.
- **Resolve every "it/this/they"** to the real noun. Keep helper words (that, who, the, a).
- **Warm, not childish.** Warmth = "you," positive contractions (you'll, it's), and concrete reassurance ("Your bag gets a numbered tag; only you can claim it"). Prefer "cannot"/"do not" over "can't"/"don't" for critical instructions, where a misread would matter.

## The tells to hunt (before → after)

| Tell | Fix |
|---|---|
| Em-dash asides | "The station — most reach it by Grab — is close." → "The station is close. Most people reach it by Grab." |
| Nominalization | "We provide storage for luggage." → "We store your luggage." |
| Hedge / flourish | "It is arguably somewhat affordable." → "It costs 50,000 VND per bag per day." |
| Throat-clearing | "It is important to note that storage closes at 10 PM." → "Storage closes at 10 PM." |
| Passive | "Bags can be dropped off from 7 AM." → "You can leave bags from 7 AM." |
| Run-on (3+ clauses) | Split into two sentences of ≤2 clauses each. |
| Phrasal verb | "drop off your bag and pick it up later" → "leave your bag and collect it later" |
| Literary metaphor | "the quiet tax on living here" → "a routine cost of living here" |
| Marketese | "an unforgettable, breathtaking journey" → the concrete facts a traveler needs |

## The 5-pass edit (run every draft through this, in order)

1. **Structure:** answer in sentence one; cut throat-clearing; chunk into short paragraphs; cut length ~half.
2. **Sentences:** split anything over ~25 words; max 2 clauses; passive → active.
3. **Words:** fancy → common; free hidden verbs; delete fillers/hedges; break noun strings.
4. **Global:** remove idioms, ambiguous phrasal verbs, humor, culture/season refs; format dates/money/times; one term per concept; resolve pronouns.
5. **De-stiff polish:** em-dashes ≤1/paragraph; break robotic symmetry ("It's not just X, it's Y"); **read it aloud** — if you would not say it to a friend at the shop counter, rewrite it; final Hemingway check (grade ≤8).

## AEO tie-in (why plain wins the machine too)

Every guide section should open with a **question-style heading** and a **40–60-word plain answer that leads with the real number** (VND + USD, minutes, hours), then expand. Answer-first, objective, short-sentence writing is exactly what Google featured snippets and AI Overviews lift — so the same edit that helps a Korean traveler also helps the crawler. Objective facts beat promotional language for extraction; one consistent term per concept strengthens the entity signal.
